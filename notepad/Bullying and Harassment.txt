Moderation flow for the Bullying and Harassment policy, part one of two.

The moderation flow for the Bullying and Harassment policy can be understood as a process divided into two major complementary stages.

The first is the analysis stage, where the initial consultation of the policy takes place and the indicators of violation are identified.

In this stage, the moderator examines the content — whether text, image, or video — and checks for signs of attacks, such as physical insults, sexualized harassment, negative comparisons, or social exclusion.

After that, the target is defined in the general context as a real person, which may be private-adult, private-minor, public-figure, adult with self-report, public-figure with purpose-exposure, among others.

The second stage is the practical part of moderation, which consists of recording in the system the classification of the type of attack and the target.

In this stage, it is essential to identify who is the target in the context of the post, which may be someone who is being mentioned and or or depicted.

The targets in the context of the post may be the reporter, parent post, parent comment owner, a person depicted in the image or video (PDITI or PDITV), owner of shared media, other, a user tagged or mentioned in the post, media caption or comment, or within media.

>ctrl + F = Establishing Targets
>Who is the target in the real context
A target in the real context is established when there is an identifiable individual in the content under review, and this does not necessarily require the person’s face to be depicted, since identification can occur through pronouns, indirect mentions, or contextual details such as relationship to the reporter, age, physical attributes, job, or location.

For example, the statement “This girl I know is a slut. She is a colleague of mine at Starbucks and always wears this silly red hat” falls within policy because it identifies a specific person. 

In contrast, content aimed at non-real or non-identifiable individuals does not violate the Bullying and Harassment policy. 

Similarly, groups defined broadly by protected characteristics, occupations, fan bases, or corporate employment are considered too large to qualify as bullying targets unless the context narrows the group to identifiable individuals. 

For instance, “Justin Bieber fans are fat and ugly” does not fall within policy, while “The Justin Bieber fan girls in my homeroom are fat and ugly” does fall within policy.

>Limited scope public figure
Limited scope public figure is an individual who technically qualifies as a public figure but whose primary fame is limited to one of the following specific scopes: activism, journalism, or those who become famous through involuntary means. Where a public figure has broader fame (e.g., both an actor and an activist), they do not qualify to be limited scope public figures.

>ctrl + F
>Involuntary Public Figure (IPF)
Involuntary Public Figure are individuals who technically qualify as public figures but have not engaged with their fame, unless they have been accused of criminal activity in the context of the incident that led to their fame. This does not include accusations of previous criminal activity or criminal records. Involuntary Public Figures are designated and communicated by Content Policy on a case by case basis. If you have a suggestion, please reach out to your GO Market Team counterpart.

>ctrl + F
>Public figures

Public figures, are defined as individuals who meet at least one of several criteria: they may be Heads of State, members of the executive or legislative branches of government at state, national, or supranational levels, political candidates running for office (up to 30 days after an election if not elected).

Public figures, are also defined as people with over one million followers on a social media account, or individuals mentioned by name in the title of five or more news articles or media pieces within the last two years, as determined by local news searches. 

Examples of public figures include Luiz Inácio, Oprah Winfrey, Trevor Noah, Cristiano Ronaldo, and Ellen DeGeneres. Importantly, minors under the age of 13 do not qualify as public figures.

>ctrl + F
>Self-Reporting
Self-Reporting occur When a name or face match has been established. A name match is established when the individual who is referenced in the content reports the content.

>ctrl + F
>Purposeful exposure
Purposeful exposure is when the poster either tags the public figure in the content or posts a comment directly on the public figure's own post or page.

Label: Sexualized Harrassment, Violent Tragedies, Calls for self-injury or suicide. Targets: Anyone.

Label: Calls for death and statements in favor of contracting or developing a medical condition. Targets: anyone with exception. Adult public figure with purposeful exposure.

Label: Claims about sexual activity. Targets: Any Minor, Private Adult, Limited scope public figure. 

Label: Claims about romantic involvement and gender identity. Targets: private Minor, private Adult with self-report, Minor involuntary public figure.

Label: Negative Comparison to animals or inanimate objects. Targets: private: Minor or Adult, Limited scope public figure, Minor public figure with purposeful exposure.

Label: Statements of inferiority about physical appearance. Targets: Anyone with Exception. Adult public figure with purposeful exposure.

Label: Targeted cursing. Targets: private Minor, private Adults with self-report, Minor involuntary public figure.

Label: Physical bullying and Derogatory terms related to female-gendered cursing. Targets: Private minors.

Label: Negative Character or Contempt or Exclusion. Targets: private Minor, private Adult with self-reported, Minor involuntary public figure.

Label: Neutral, positive physical descriptions or non-negative character or ability claims, when self-reported. Targets: private Minor or Adult, and Minor Involuntary public figure.

Label: Other, when Allegations about criminal or illegal behavior. Targets: any Minor.

Label: Other, when Threats to release an individual's private phone number, residential address, or email address. Targets: Anyone.
