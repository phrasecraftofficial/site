>Self-Reporting
When a name/face match has been established. A name match is established when the individual who is referenced in the content reports the content.

>ctrl + F
>Limited scope public figure
An individual who technically qualifies as a public figure but whose primary fame is limited to one of the following specific scopes: activism, journalism, or those who become famous through involuntary means. Where a public figure has broader fame (e.g., both an actor and an activist), they do not qualify to be limited scope public figures.

>ctrl + F
>Involuntary Public Figure (IPF)
Individuals who technically qualify as public figures but have not engaged with their fame, unless they have been accused of criminal activity in the context of the incident that led to their fame. This does not include accusations of previous criminal activity or criminal records. Involuntary Public Figures are designated and communicated by Content Policy on a case by case basis. If you have a suggestion, please reach out to your GO Market Team counterpart.

>ctrl + F
>Public figures
1. Public figures must meet at least one of the following criteria:
a. Heads of State, or
b. Members of the executive or legislative branches of government at state, national, or supranational level, or
c. Political candidates running for office (for up to 30 days after election if candidate is not elected to position), or
d. People that have 1M+ fans or followers on one of their social media accounts, or
e. People who are mentioned by name in the title of 5 or more news articles or media pieces within the last 2 years, as determined by local news search
f. For example:
i. Jair Bolsonaro (former president of Brazil)
ii. Oprah Winfrey (Celebrity)
iii. Trevor Noah (Comedian)
iv. Cristiano Ronaldo (Football player)
v. Ellen DeGeneres (Actress and TV show host)
2. Note: Minors under the age of 13 don't qualify as public figures

>ctrl + F
>Purposeful exposure
Purposeful exposure is when the poster either tags the public figure in the content or posts a comment directly on the public figure's own post or page.

>ctrl + F
>Establishing Targets
A target is established when there's an identifiable individual in the content in question. 
That doesn't necessarily mean that the face of the individual(s) being targeted has to be depicted in the content in order for it to contain an identifiable target given that there are other ways to identify a person.
This includes usage of pronouns or mentioning an individual (not by name), and additional identifying details/context such as relationship to reporter, age, physical attributes, job, location, and so on.
i. For example: "This girl I know is a slut. She is a colleague of mine at Starbucks and always wears this silly red hat" (does fall within policy).
Content that targets non-real or non-identifiable individuals is not in violation of the Bullying and Harassment policy.
Groups of individuals defined by protected characteristics, occupation, fan-groups, or employment by a corporation are too big of a group to qualify as a bullying target unless there is context that specifically limits the size of the group.
ii. "Justin Bieber fans are fat and ugly" = Doesn't fall within policy
iii. "The Justin Bieber fan girls in my homeroom are fat and ugly" = Does fall within policy
